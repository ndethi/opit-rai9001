% ----- Abstract -----
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Low-resource African languages face a critical preservation challenge as traditional knowledge risks being lost in the digital age. This thesis presents thiLLMo, an Ontology-Grounded Retrieval Augmented Generation (OG-RAG) system addressing the challenge of culturally faithful translation for Kikuyu proverbs—a vital component of intangible cultural heritage. Kikuyu proverbs (\emph{thimo}) encode centuries of accumulated knowledge about social systems, economic practices, ethics, and governance that cannot be preserved through simple lexical translation.

The research addresses a fundamental question: How can we achieve culturally faithful translation of low-resource language proverbs when confronted with both data scarcity (inherent to underserved languages) and the need for deep cultural grounding (beyond current Large Language Model capabilities)? Unlike conventional RAG systems that rely on unstructured vector similarity, thiLLMo integrates a formal cultural ontology comprising 959 concepts and 6,445 relationships with Large Language Models to enable culturally grounded translation.

The system employs a rigorous comparative evaluation design assessing three approaches: raw LLM translation, traditional RAG, and the proposed OG-RAG architecture. Evaluation on 100 Kikuyu proverbs from the wealth and prosperity domain demonstrates statistically significant improvements: 10.5\% in cultural authenticity ($p < 0.001$) and 19.8\% in overall translation fidelity compared to baseline approaches. The OG-RAG architecture achieves 24.9\% improvement over traditional RAG in cultural authenticity scores and 43.7\% improvement over raw LLM in metaphorical preservation.

This work contributes both a novel OG-RAG architecture for cultural translation and practical evidence that structured cultural knowledge can enhance AI systems while maintaining community ownership and cultural sovereignty. The methodology demonstrates that community-centered development is technically superior—expert validation during ontology construction directly improves system accuracy. The research establishes viable pathways for sophisticated AI applications in low-resource languages without requiring massive training corpora, offering a scalable model for AI equity and cultural preservation in underserved communities globally.
