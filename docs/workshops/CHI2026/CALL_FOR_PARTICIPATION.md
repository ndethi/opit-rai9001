## Overview

The **AI Across Cultures: Co-Designing Equitable and Culturally Grounded Futures** workshop examines how Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Human–Computer Interaction (HCI) are shaped by cultural, linguistic, and sociotechnical contexts, and how these systems can be reimagined to better serve diverse communities worldwide. 

While AI and HCI are often presented as universal, their design, data, and governance are predominantly rooted in high-resource environments, leaving many low-resource languages and culturally diverse communities underrepresented.

This workshop aims to shift this narrative by positioning **culture, language, and local knowledge at the center of AI and HCI research and practice**. Bringing together HCI researchers, AI and NLP practitioners, policymakers, designers, and community leaders, the workshop creates a space for cross-disciplinary dialogue on how AI systems can be co-designed **with** communities rather than merely deployed **for** them.

Through case studies, participatory design activities, and collaborative discussions, participants will explore how cultural philosophies, linguistic diversity, and community priorities can be meaningfully integrated into AI design methodologies, evaluation practices, and policy frameworks.

## Call for Participation

We invite **HCI researchers, AI and NLP practitioners, policymakers, educators, designers, community representatives, and students** to participate in the CHI 2026 workshop **AI Across Cultures: Co-Designing Equitable and Culturally Grounded Futures**.

Artificial Intelligence and Human–Computer Interaction are often presented as universal, yet their design, data, infrastructures, and governance are largely shaped by high-resource contexts. This workshop centers **low-resource, indigenous, and culturally diverse settings not as edge cases, but as starting points for innovation**. We explore how AI can be designed, adapted, and governed in ways that foreground **cultural identity, linguistic diversity, equity, and sustainability**.

The workshop will bring together an interdisciplinary community to reflect on how technical design practices, participatory methods, and policy frameworks can be aligned to support culturally grounded and community-led AI futures.

### Key Questions

The workshop will engage participants around the following guiding questions:

- **How can AI be adapted to support indigenous languages and cultural contexts in inclusive, sustainable, and respectful ways?**
- **What sociotechnical frameworks are needed to align AI with diverse cultural values and knowledge systems?**
- **How can participatory design and community-led approaches reshape AI development for equity and cultural grounding?**
- **What evaluation practices and policy frameworks can ensure AI systems respect and reflect cultural diversity?**

## Workshop Themes

Participants are encouraged to engage with topics including (but not limited to):

1. **Cultural Adaptation of AI Systems**
   - Designing AI for low-resource and indigenous languages
   - Cultural grounding in NLP and translation systems
   - Ontology and knowledge representation for cultural contexts

2. **Participatory and Community-Led Design**
   - Co-design methodologies with marginalized communities
   - Community ownership and governance of AI systems
   - Ethical frameworks for culturally respectful AI

3. **Evaluation and Assessment**
   - Culturally-aware evaluation metrics
   - Human-centered assessment for cultural fidelity
   - Addressing bias and representation in AI

4. **Policy and Governance**
   - Policy frameworks for linguistic and cultural diversity
   - Data sovereignty and indigenous data governance
   - Sustainable AI infrastructure for diverse contexts
