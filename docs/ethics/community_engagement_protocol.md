## 1. PROJECT OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Purpose
This protocol governs the ethical collection, documentation, and use of Kikuyu cultural knowledge in the form of traditional proverbs (thimo cia Gĩkũyũ) for:
- Cultural preservation and education
- Machine translation system development
- Academic research (Master's thesis)
- Community benefit and knowledge transmission

### 1.2 Philosophical Commitments
- **Cultural Realism**: Kikuyu cultural concepts have real existence and validity
- **Community Authority**: Kikuyu cultural experts are the authoritative knowledge source
- **Non-Universalism**: Kikuyu concepts may be incommensurable with Western categories
- **Reciprocity**: Knowledge sharing must benefit the community
- **Sovereignty**: Community retains control over cultural knowledge

### 1.3 Guiding Principles
1. **Free, Prior, and Informed Consent (FPIC)** - UNESCO Indigenous data sovereignty
2. **Benefit-sharing** - Community shares in outcomes
3. **Cultural Respect** - Honor sacred/restricted knowledge boundaries
4. **Attribution** - Acknowledge knowledge holders appropriately
5. **Right to Withdraw** - Participants can withdraw at any time
6. **Data Protection** - Secure storage and ethical use

## 4. DATA COLLECTION PROTOCOLS

### 4.1 Session Structure

**Pre-Session (1 week before):**
- Send list of topics/themes to be discussed
- Allow expert time to recall relevant proverbs
- Respect cultural preparation practices
- Confirm scheduling and location

**Session Structure (1-2 hours):**
1. **Opening (10 min)**
   - Cultural greetings and relationship building
   - Review session objectives
   - Confirm consent and comfort level

2. **Proverb Collection (30-40 min)**
   - Expert shares proverbs on theme
   - Document Kikuyu text (audio recording + written)
   - Immediate clarification of pronunciation/spelling

3. **Cultural Explanation (30-40 min)**
   - Literal translation
   - Cultural meaning and significance
   - Usage contexts and appropriateness
   - Related concepts and presuppositions

4. **Closing (10 min)**
   - Review what was documented
   - Opportunity for corrections
   - Schedule next session
   - Provide compensation

**Post-Session:**
- Transcribe and document within 24 hours
- Send summary to expert for review
- Address any corrections promptly
- Prepare for next session

### 4.2 Documentation Standards

**For Each Proverb:**
1. **Kikuyu text** (verified spelling)
2. **Phonetic transcription** (if needed)
3. **Literal translation** (word-by-word)
4. **Expert translation** (culturally-informed)
5. **Cultural meaning** (deep explanation)
6. **Moral instruction** (teaching purpose)
7. **Usage contexts** (when/where/who/why)
8. **Metaphorical structure** (if applicable)
9. **Related concepts** (presupposed knowledge)
10. **Regional variations** (if any)
11. **Usage frequency** (expert estimate)
12. **Cultural weight** (significance rating)

**For Each Cultural Concept:**
1. **Kikuyu term**
2. **Morphological analysis**
3. **Semantic dimensions** (multiple meanings)
4. **English approximation** (best translation)
5. **Translation inadequacies** (what's lost)
6. **Cultural notes** (worldview context)
7. **Presupposed concepts** (prerequisites)
8. **Historical context**
9. **Modern relevance**

### 4.3 Sacred Knowledge Protocols

**Before Each Session:**
- Ask if there are any restrictions on shared knowledge
- Clarify what can/cannot be published
- Respect community protocols

**Red Flags (Stop and Clarify):**
- Expert hesitates or seems uncomfortable
- Knowledge relates to initiation ceremonies
- Expert mentions "this is only for elders"
- Religious/spiritual sensitive content
- Personal family/clan specific knowledge

**Sacred Knowledge Handling:**
- Do not record if expert indicates restriction
- Mark as "restricted" in any notes taken
- Delete recordings if requested
- Seek community elder approval if uncertain
- When in doubt, exclude from public dataset

## 5. VALIDATION AND QUALITY ASSURANCE

### 5.1 Expert Validation Process

**Stage 1: Primary Documentation**
- Ireri documents proverbs and meanings
- Full cultural context provided
- Initial cultural weight estimates

**Stage 2: Review and Refinement**
- Ireri reviews transcribed documentation
- Corrections and clarifications made
- Additional context added as needed

**Stage 3: Secondary Validation (if possible)**
- Seek 2-3 additional Kikuyu cultural experts
- Cross-validate interpretations
- Measure inter-rater agreement (target: >0.80)
- Resolve discrepancies through discussion

**Stage 4: Community Review (if feasible)**
- Present completed ontology to Kikuyu community
- Gather feedback on accuracy and appropriateness
- Make adjustments based on community input

### 5.2 Validation Limitations Documentation

**Current Limitations:**
- Single expert validator (Ireri) vs. ideal multi-expert panel
- Limited geographic representation (single region)
- Time constraints preventing extensive community review
- Resource limitations for compensation of multiple experts

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Clearly document single-expert limitation in thesis
- Seek secondary validation for high-weight concepts (wealth, poverty)
- Prioritize quality over quantity (fewer well-validated proverbs)
- Leave framework for future multi-expert expansion

**Thesis Transparency:**
- Acknowledge validation limitations in methodology
- Report single-expert validation approach
- Discuss implications for generalizability
- Propose future work with multi-expert validation

### 7.1 Storage and Access

**During Research:**
- Encrypted storage of all recordings and documents
- Password-protected files
- Backup to secure cloud storage
- Access limited to research team only

**After Research:**
- Long-term preservation in institutional repository
- Public dataset excludes personal information
- Restricted dataset available only with approval
- Sacred/restricted knowledge permanently excluded

### 7.2 Data Retention

**Audio Recordings:**
- Retained for 5 years post-thesis
- Deleted upon expert request
- Not shared publicly

**Transcriptions and Documentation:**
- Indefinite retention for cultural preservation
- Public access to non-restricted proverbs
- Attribution maintained permanently

**Personal Information:**
- Stored separately from cultural data
- Deleted after consent period expires
- Shared only with explicit permission

## 9. CULTURAL SENSITIVITY GUIDELINES

### 9.1 Researcher Conduct

**Respectful Engagement:**
- Learn basic Kikuyu greetings and cultural protocols
- Show humility and openness to learning
- Avoid imposing Western academic frameworks
- Listen more than speak in cultural discussions

**Cultural Humility:**
- Recognize limitations of non-Kikuyu perspective
- Defer to expert on cultural interpretations
- Avoid assumptions about meanings
- Ask for clarification rather than assume

**Relationship Building:**
- Invest time in building trust
- Show genuine interest in culture
- Share researcher's own background
- Maintain ongoing communication

### 9.2 Language and Terminology

**Preferred Terms:**
- "Kikuyu cultural expert" not "informant" or "subject"
- "Knowledge holder" not "data source"
- "Collaboration" not "extraction"
- "Traditional knowledge" not "folklore"

**Respectful Documentation:**
- Use Kikuyu terms in addition to English
- Preserve original language structure
- Avoid oversimplification
- Maintain cultural context
