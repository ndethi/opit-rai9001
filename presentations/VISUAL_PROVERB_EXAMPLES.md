# Visual Proverb Examples: The Futility of Literal Translation

**Purpose:** Demonstrate why literal translation fails for culturally rich Kikuyu proverbs  
**Intended Use:** Presentation slides with visual imagery  
**Selection Criteria:** Proverbs with strong visual metaphors (animals, nature, physical objects)

## SLIDE 1: The Stork and the Locusts

### Kikuyu Original
**"Aikaragia mbia ta njuu ngigi"**

### Literal Word-by-Word Translation
"He-looks-after money like storks locusts"

### Expert Translation (Cultural)
**"He looks after his money the way storks pursue locusts."**

### Cultural Meaning
"Whoever has much always wants more" - Teaching about greed, insatiability, and possessiveness

### Visual Metaphor
🦩 **Stork** (njuu) - A bird known for relentlessly chasing food  
🦗 **Locusts** (ngigi) - Abundant insects constantly in motion  
💰 **Money** (mbia) - The object of obsessive pursuit

### Why Literal Translation Fails

**Raw GPT-4 Attempt (BLEU: 4.52):**  
"One does not hunt game by chasing after it."

**❌ FAILURE:**
- Lost the stork metaphor entirely (changed to "hunting game")
- Lost the locust imagery (changed to generic "chasing")
- Lost the money connection (changed to hunting)
- Lost the cultural teaching about greed
- COMPLETELY DIFFERENT METAPHOR

**OG-RAG Translation (BLEU: 9.03):**  
"He guards his wealth as a stork chases locusts."

**✅ SUCCESS:**
- ✓ Preserves stork imagery
- ✓ Preserves locust chasing
- ✓ Maintains wealth focus ("guards his wealth")
- ✓ Captures relentless pursuit concept
- Different words, same cultural meaning

### Presentation Visual
**Image:** Split screen showing:
- LEFT: Stork chasing locusts in African savanna
- RIGHT: Person obsessively counting money
- CAPTION: "The metaphor IS the meaning"

## SLIDE 2: The Empty Granary

### Kikuyu Original
**"Cia thuguri itiyuragia ikumbi"**

### Literal Word-by-Word Translation
"Things-bought not-fill granary"

### Expert Translation (Cultural)
**"Bought things do not fill the granary."**

### Cultural Meaning
"One must work hard to achieve wealth" - Teaching against begging/dependency; must cultivate own fields

### Visual Metaphor
🌾 **Granary** (ikumbi) - Traditional storage for harvested grain  
🛒 **Bought things** (cia thuguri) - Items acquired without personal labor  
👨‍🌾 **Implied cultivation** - The missing element: personal farming effort

### Why Literal Translation Fails

**Raw GPT-4 Attempt (BLEU: 5.41):**  
"The ways of a thief make him walk at night."

**❌ FAILURE:**
- Lost granary imagery entirely (changed to "walking at night")
- Lost bought vs cultivated distinction (changed to "thief")
- Lost agricultural metaphor (no farming reference)
- Lost self-reliance teaching
- COMPLETELY UNRELATED PROVERB

**OG-RAG Translation (BLEU: 36.56):**  
"Borrowed seeds do not fill the granary."

**✅ PARTIAL SUCCESS:**
- ✓ Preserves granary imagery
- ✓ Maintains agricultural context
- ✓ Captures dependency vs self-reliance theme
- ✓ Cultural teaching preserved
- "Borrowed seeds" culturally equivalent to "bought things"

### Presentation Visual
**Image:** Traditional Kikuyu granary (raised storage structure)
- EMPTY granary labeled "Bought things"
- FULL granary labeled "Cultivated harvest"
- CAPTION: "No shortcuts to prosperity"

## SLIDE 3: The Dying Goat

### Kikuyu Original
**"Gitonga kiriaga munyuko"**

### Literal Word-by-Word Translation
"Rich-person eats dying-goat"

### Expert Translation (Cultural)
**"A wealthy man eats only a dying goat (very thin with tough meat/undesirable food)."**

### Cultural Meaning
"Relentless quest for wealth can often be meaningless" - Rich person so obsessed with accumulating wealth they neglect their own nourishment and wellbeing

### Visual Metaphor
🐐 **Dying goat** (munyuko) - Emaciated animal with tough, inedible meat  
🤑 **Wealthy man** (gitonga) - Person with material riches  
🍽️ **Ironic eating** - Having wealth but consuming worst food

### Why Literal Translation Fails

**Raw GPT-4 Attempt (BLEU: 1.41):**  
"Wealth announces itself in a homestead."

**❌ FAILURE:**
- Lost the dying goat metaphor entirely
- Lost the ironic eating imagery
- Lost the self-neglect teaching
- Changed to celebration of wealth display
- OPPOSITE MEANING

**OG-RAG Translation (BLEU: 0.93):**  
"In chasing riches, one neglects to feast; prosperity without nourishment breeds hollowness."

**✅ SUCCESS:**
- ✓ Captures self-neglect theme
- ✓ Preserves irony of wealth without wellbeing
- ✓ Cultural teaching about meaningless pursuit
- Different metaphor, same wisdom

### Presentation Visual

- Rich man surrounded by gold coins

## SLIDE 4: The Bridewealth Goat

### Kikuyu Original
**"Gutiri mburi itangiracio, tiga inawathe"**

### Literal Word-by-Word Translation
"There-is-no goat unsuitable-for-bridewealth unless it-has-defect"

### Expert Translation (Cultural)
**"Any goat can serve as bridewealth, unless it has a defect."**

### Cultural Meaning
"The value of a wife is very great and can only be symbolized by the best choice" - Teaching that marriage requires presenting the BEST gifts to in-laws, showing deep respect and value

### Visual Metaphor
🐐 **Goat** (mburi) - Traditional wealth symbol and bridewealth currency  
💍 **Bridewealth** (tangiracio) - Gifts given to bride's family  
✨ **Perfection** (no defect) - Must be flawless to show proper respect  
👰 **Wife's value** - Symbolized by quality of gift

### Why Literal Translation Fails

**Raw GPT-4 Attempt (BLEU: 5.67):**  
"A homestead that does not have goats cannot hold a bridewealth ceremony, unless it has a defect."

**❌ FAILURE:**
- Changed focus to lacking goats (poverty) vs quality of goats
- Lost the "best quality" teaching
- Lost the symbolism of wife's value
- Confused the logical relationship
- Grammatically awkward

**OG-RAG Translation (BLEU: 2.63):**  
"For marriage, even the smallest gift holds meaning, but never offer what is broken."

**✅ SUCCESS:**
- ✓ Captures marriage context
- ✓ Preserves gift-giving requirement
- ✓ Maintains quality/respect teaching
- ✓ Cultural principle clear
- Different imagery, same values

### Presentation Visual
**Image:** Beautiful healthy goats with ribbons
- One perfect goat highlighted with ✓
- One defective goat with ✗
- Traditional Kikuyu wedding ceremony background
- CAPTION: "Only the best to honor worth"

## SLIDE 5: The Homestead with Goats

### Kikuyu Original
**"Mucii uri mburi ndwagaga kihuno"**

### Literal Word-by-Word Translation
"Homestead with goats not-lacks miscarriage"

### Expert Translation (Cultural)
**"A homestead with goats does not lack miscarriages."**

### Cultural Meaning
"The good and the bad go together" - Teaching that wealth brings both blessings and setbacks; accept everything in stride

### Visual Metaphor
🏡 **Homestead** (mucii) - Family compound/household  
🐐 **Goats** (mburi) - Symbol of wealth and prosperity  
😔 **Miscarriage** (kihuno) - Inevitable loss/setback  
⚖️ **Balance** - Good fortune accompanied by misfortune

### Why Literal Translation Fails

**Raw GPT-4 Attempt (BLEU: 5.82):**  
"Home is where the heart feels most at ease."

**❌ FAILURE:**
- Lost goats entirely (changed to abstract "heart")
- Lost miscarriage metaphor (changed to "at ease")
- Lost wealth and setback teaching
- Changed to sentimental platitude
- OPPOSITE MESSAGE (ease vs inevitable hardship)

**OG-RAG Translation (BLEU: 38.16):**  
"Where there is prosperity, there is also loss; wealth does not exempt one from sorrow."

**✅ SUCCESS:**
- ✓ Captures prosperity (goats = wealth)
- ✓ Preserves inevitable loss theme (miscarriage = sorrow)
- ✓ Maintains balance teaching
- ✓ Cultural wisdom clear
- Abstract phrasing, concrete meaning

### Presentation Visual
**Image:** Traditional Kikuyu homestead showing duality:
- Healthy goats grazing (prosperity)
- Family experiencing loss (sorrow)
- Yin-yang symbol overlay
- CAPTION: "Wealth brings both joy and trials"

## PRESENTATION TALKING POINTS

### Key Messages

1. **Visual metaphors are not decorative—they ARE the meaning**
   - Storks chasing locusts = relentless pursuit (can't replace with "hunting game")
   - Granary filling = prosperity measure (can't replace with "walking at night")
   - Dying goat = ironic suffering (can't replace with "wealth display")

2. **Literal translation destroys cultural knowledge**
   - Word-for-word translation produces gibberish or wrong meaning
   - Cultural context required to select appropriate metaphors
   - Standard MT systems lack this cultural grounding

3. **BLEU scores mislead for cultural translation**
   - Highest BLEU (68.04) still has wrong cultural teaching
   - Low BLEU (9.03) can be culturally faithful
   - Need new metrics prioritizing meaning over word overlap

4. **OG-RAG preserves cultural wisdom through structured knowledge**
   - Ontology provides cultural context for metaphors
   - Knowledge graph links proverbs to cultural concepts
   - Result: Different words, same wisdom
